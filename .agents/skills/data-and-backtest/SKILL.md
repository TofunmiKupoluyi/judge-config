# The data, the panel contract and the battery

This is how research and backtests run against the data. Raw candles, funding rates and exchange metrics come from the S3 bucket described in the `binance-futures-data` skill. The data catalogue is not a delivered artifact: it is the bucket's meta files (`manifest.json`, `symbols.parquet`, `metrics_symbols.parquet`) plus the panel contract in §2 with its unprovisioned flags.

## 1. Raw data
**Candles.** 1-minute, 1-hour and 1-day OHLCV for every Binance USDT-M perpetual that ever listed (survivorship-free: delisted names stay), from the S3 bucket in the `binance-futures-data` skill — bucket layout, schemas, credentials (`AWS_S3_ACCESS_KEY` / `AWS_S3_ACCESS_KEY_SECRET`, read-only, never printed or logged) and pitfalls are documented there and are not repeated here. Two facts that skill states and research must respect: missing minutes are real gaps, never filled with synthetic prices (a bar with `n_trades == 0` is a real quiet bar; leave gaps NaN), and prices are last-traded, not mark — Binance drives liquidations and funding off mark price, so liquidation modelling from candles is approximate.
**Funding.** Also from the bucket: one row per settlement — `time`, `funding_rate`, `interval_hours` (point-in-time: 8 h, 4 h, 1 h as declared by the exchange at that time; 74% of the 2026 universe is on 4 h). Sum rates over the window rather than assuming an interval; a position pays only at the settlement instant.
**Exchange metrics.** Also from the bucket, 5-minute from September 2020: open interest (contracts and $) plus long/short and taker ratios; the panels expose them as `oi_value`, `global_ls_acct`, `taker_ls_vol`, `toptrader_ls_acct`, `toptrader_ls_pos`. 0 open interest is a data gap, never a value: treat as missing and forward-fill. Metrics coverage starts later than candles and has more gaps — check `meta/metrics_symbols.parquet` before assuming a period exists.
**Inputs not in the bucket — currently provisioned nowhere.** Until these are provisioned (and announced in the catalogue sense above), any panel column or rule derived from them cannot be built, and a hypothesis that needs one has the hard blocker "needs data not in the catalogue": escalate (AGENTS.md §7) and propose elsewhere.
- Market cap (`mcap`) — no source in the bucket.
- Premium index klines (mark − index, 1-minute) — the bucket explicitly excludes mark/index price.
- Symbol tags beyond `meta/symbols.parquet`: that file gives contract type and first/last candle, but not underlying type (COIN / INDEX / …), contract multiplier (1000PEPE-style names) or redenomination events — so the universe's non-COIN exclusion and redenomination NaN rule need it provisioned.
- Hourly aggtrade and book-depth aggregates (trade-size buckets, ±1% / ±5% depth).

## 2. The panel interface (the contract the battery supplies and signal code is written against)
These definitions are binding on both sides: Scout's feature definitions assume them, and the battery you run must supply a `panel` object with exactly these semantics, derived from the S3 bucket (`binance-futures-data` skill). Panels are wide frames: index = timestamp (UTC), columns = symbols; float32. `signal.py` touches data only through the `panel` object passed to `build()` (accessor list in the `research-scout` skill), never through storage or the network.
- **Hourly** (the base resolution; 1-minute bars aggregated by OPEN time of the hour, stamped at the hour's CLOSE so a row at 13:00 contains 12:00–12:59): `close, open, high, low, qv (quote volume), tbq (taker-buy quote), n_trades, fund (funding paid at print hours, 0 elsewhere; sign: positive = longs pay), fund_interval, mdv21 (21-day median of daily quote volume, forward-filled from the daily series), beta_btc (rolling 60-day beta of hourly returns to BTC, clipped [−3, 3], lagged one hour), mcap (carried forward, 2025-Q3 onward; unprovisioned — §1), univ (bool: hysteresis universe), no_entry (bool: delisting ramp), oi_value, premium (unprovisioned — §1), taker/positioning ratios`.
- **Daily** (00:00 UTC; day D's value is stamped at D+1 00:00 so that "data at day t" means data strictly before t 00:00): `close_d, lr_d (log return), rs_d (simple return), resid_s_d / resid_l_d (BTC-residual simple/log return using yesterday's beta), fund_d (funding a long pays per calendar day), mdv, mcap (unprovisioned — §1), univ`.
- **1-minute** data has no wide panels and is reachable only per symbol through `panel.minute(symbol, start, end)`: signal code must aggregate immediately to the feature it needs and release the frame. The battery still evaluates at hourly or daily horizons.
- **Universe**: enter when MDV21 ≥ $3M, stay while ≥ $2M (hysteresis); no entries in the last 14 days of a symbol's data, membership ends 7 days before the data ends (delisting-announcement proxy, disclosed); non-COIN underlyings excluded; BTCUSDT is never a signal name (it is the hedge leg).

## 3. Point-in-time rules (your VERIFY step checks these line by line)
1. A feature for day t uses only rows with timestamp < t 00:00 UTC; for an hourly feature at bar close h, rows with timestamp ≤ h (the bar that closes at h is known at h).
2. `daily_from_hourly(frame, how)` stamps day D at D+1 00:00 and reindexes to `panel.dates`; never shift it again.
3. Funding is known at its print; the NEXT funding rate is known from the premium index only approximately (the nowcast failed its gate — E74), so it is not a feature unless the proposal names it as such.
4. Universe and beta are lagged; mcap is carried forward (stale by construction — say so when used).
5. Listings: a name has no feature until its lookback is full; do not backfill. Redenominations: the tags data gives the date; returns across it are NaN.
6. Anything that reads the eval window to choose a parameter is lookahead in the research sense, even if it is point-in-time in the data sense.

## 4. The fixed battery (you run it; no agent edits it)
**Target.** Next-7-day cross-sectional BTC-residual SIMPLE return, net of funding (Σ daily simple residuals over the horizon minus funding paid), on Monday cross-sections of the universe. Variants selected by the hypothesis's horizon class: `xs-1d` (daily cross-sections, next-1-day), `mft-1h/4h/12h` (hourly cross-sections sampled every H hours, next-H-hour residual return; the MFT battery adds breakeven round-trip cost in bp).
**Windows.** calib 2022-01-01 → 2023-12-31; eval 2024-01-01 → EVAL_END, where EVAL_END = last completed month end at the time the hypothesis is frozen for testing, recorded on the ticket and asserted by the battery (it is never extended by an agent). A window with fewer than 20 weeks carrying the feature is PENDING-DATA, never a verdict.
**Gates, both windows unless stated.** K1 weekly rank-IC t: calib ≥ 2.5 and eval same sign with t ≥ 1.5. K2 quintile monotonicity: Spearman of window-average quintile mean returns vs rank ≥ 0.8. K3 residual IC after regressing the feature on a4, a12, a90x_ratio, log mdv21, log mcap ≥ 50% of raw IC. K4 placebos: fixed random relabelling of names |t| < 1.5; lead/lag symmetry — the signal led by L days (L = lookback + 8, rounded up to whole weeks) must not predict better than the same signal lagged by L; a placebo that cannot be computed FAILS. K5 Q5−Q1 weekly spread in simple returns net of funding and of 20 bp × turnover ≥ 40 bp/week.
**Verdict.** CANDIDATE = K1–K5 both windows. WATCH = K1–K4 pass, K5 fails ("ranks without dollars"). RESEARCH FURTHER = calib passes K1/K2/K5, eval has the right sign with t ≥ 1.0 AND eval net dollars ≥ 0. Everything else REJECT. Orientation is +1 only: a negative result is REJECT, never flipped (the counter-signal rule is a separate, counted, post-hoc hypothesis).
**Also produced, never a gate — the standalone strategy run.** Every tested hypothesis is also traded as a sleeve: each Monday long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile of the universe (equal-weight, or z-score-weighted if pre-registered), 1× gross of notional, held one week, BTC-hedged to beta 0, net of funding and 20 bp per unit turnover (daily rebalance for `xs-1d`; hourly cross-sections for `mft-*`). Outputs: a daily series (`ret, gross, delta, beta, turnover, req_turnover, costs, funding`) and, for intraday horizons, an hourly NAV; headline metrics per calendar year and per window (return, Sharpe, Sortino, max DD, worst day, hit rate, turnover, cost drag); the master's Sharpe with a 10% overlay; quintile means; long/short leg; MDV-tercile split. `tools/backtest_report.py` turns the daily series into the standard backtest exhibit (per year → monthly → $10M metric table → key-metric panels → charts; no note box).
**Cost in the battery.** 20 bp round trip per unit turnover at the weekly horizon (conservative relative to the measured 3–8 bp all-in; the 2× stress happens at the portfolio stage).

## 5. What a survivor faces next (your portfolio stage)
A battery survivor is replayed inside a replica of the production book: hourly loop, fills at the hour's close paying fee + half-spread tier + impact, funding per print, BTC hedge, every parameter from `reference/RISK_FRAMEWORK.json`. Claims are paired against a named baseline (Δ return/yr, Newey-West t, ΔSharpe with bootstrap CI, per-year table) at 1× and 2× costs, with governor-dependent claims read at the master's imposed risk path.
**Adoption bar** (fixed): Δ ≥ 0 in BOTH windows at the master's risk budget, eval NW t ≥ 1.5, survives 2× costs, no year more than 3 points worse than the baseline. Passing = CANDIDATE dossier for a human; it is not adoption. A sleeve that cannot plausibly clear this at 2× costs is a note, not a candidate.

## 6. Execution realism carried into research (measured live, 2026-10-09 post-trade review)
Fill at the hourly close is unbiased (+2.5 ± 3.5 bp vs the research entry); realised fee 2.5–2.75 bp (model 4); realised spread+impact 0.4 bp overall against 4.4 modelled, by tier illiquid +5.9 / mid −9.7 / liquid +0.9 vs 8.7 / 3.8 / 1.1 ex fee; OPENs fill ~14 bp better than the bar close, CLOSEs ~23 bp worse (exits chase an intra-hour move); impact at < 0.5% participation ≈ 1.8 bp; everything above $1M notional is model-only (no slice has ever been split). The 2× cost stress remains the standard; a kind-dependent cost (cheaper entries, dearer exits) is a disclosed sensitivity, not a default. The +24 h markout of live trades against the replica's own markouts is the execution-side alpha monitor.

## 7. Who runs what
Scout proposes and revises; you critique, gate, freeze the pre-registration, verify the submitted code, run the battery and the standalone strategy, post the verdict and the exhibit, and carry survivors through §5. The build/battery/verdict stages are wired by the harness author against the contract in §2 and §4; until a stage is wired, tickets wait in its status and you never improvise it. The ledger lives in Linear (every `hypothesis` issue is one row; the `PREREG` comment is the count event; your recount of N binds).
