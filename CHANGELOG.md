# Changelog

## 2026-10-12 — redesign v2: config-driven engine, fixed bar, four statuses
- Judge now RUNS instead of critiquing: on a ticket in Idea Generated it extracts the newest trade config (`tools/extract_config.py`), validates it, runs `tools/judge_run.py` once (calibration-only sweep of Scout's declared ranges/alternatives → chosen config on the full sample under the production risk framework → verification battery → seven-line pass bar), posts the headline-first result with the HTML exhibit attached, and moves the ticket (PASS → Human Review; FAIL → Critiqued, or Discarded after round 5).
- New engine: `tools/data.py` (point-in-time daily fields from the S3 layout), `tools/engine.py` (signal DSL, positions, risk framework, costs, funding, CPPI governor, BTC hedge), `tools/judge_run.py`, `tools/validate_config.py`, `reference/TRADE_CONFIG_SCHEMA.json`, `reference/PASS_BAR.md`, `examples/` (three reference sleeves + the worked example), `tests/test_engine.py` (offline: noise → FAIL, planted premium → PASS).
- The result comment carries a code-written **For Scout** diagnosis in a fixed vocabulary; Judge adds a short reading and never parameter advice.
- Removed: the CRITIQUE/PREREG/VERIFY/VERDICT stages, the K1–K5 battery spec (`data-and-backtest` skill), `REPORT_FORMAT.md`, `DOSSIER_FORMAT.md`, `REJECTION_PATTERNS.md`. Added `WHAT_FAILED_BEFORE.md`, `TICKET_FORMAT.md` (identical in scout-config), the `engine-and-bar` skill, `requirements.txt`.
- `AGENTS.md`, `research-judge` skill and README rewritten for the new flow. Linear tool protocol unchanged.

## 2026-10-12 (later) — headline without the governor; warm-up excluded
- The first real ticket showed long flat stretches in the governed NAV (CPPI multiplier at its 5 % floor for 300–540 days once a losing sleeve passes ~9 % drawdown) and 64 warm-up days with no positions at the start of calibration. `judge_run.py` now sweeps and reports the headline on the risk framework WITHOUT the drawdown governor (caps, hedge, costs, funding), starts statistics on the first traded day (warm-up counted and excluded), and reports the governed run as a separate "Deployable" line (Sharpe, max DD, avg multiplier, floor-day share, longest floor run). Bar line 3 now reads the governed run: max DD ≤ 10 % and ≤ 25 % of days at the floor. `PASS_BAR.md`, `engine-and-bar`, `TICKET_FORMAT.md` (both repos) updated; `tests/test_engine.py` planted bucket uses a tercile-flat premium.

## 2026-10-12 (decision) — no drawdown governor in the harness; max DD line 20 %
- Dominik's decision: remove both the step ladder and the CPPI governor from the agentic-harness backtest for now (everything else unchanged: caps, max-loss tiers, gross cap, BTC hedge, cost model, funding). The "Deployable (governed)" line and the floor-share rule are gone; bar line 3 is max drawdown ≤ 20 % on the governor-free run (provisional). Drawdown control is handled at deployment, after Human Review.

## 2026-10-12 (evening) — the headline engine is Obsidian's hourly production replica
- Dominik's decision: the harness backtest must be Obsidian's engine, hourly, minus the ladder and CPPI. `tools/obsidian_engine.py` is a verbatim copy of `backtest/engine_v2.py` (v2.3.1); `tools/panels.py` builds the hourly panels from the bucket with Obsidian's exact definitions (open-time stamps, funding at the floor hour, mdv21, hysteresis universe + delisting ramp, 60-day hourly beta, caps from the daily OI file; non-coin perps excluded via `reference/NON_COIN_SYMBOLS.json`); `engine.run_hourly` hands the DSL's daily targets to the engine as its `extra` panel (stamped D−1 23:00, held 24 h, constant 8 bp edge for the entry hurdle). `judge_run.py` sweeps and reports on it; full sample = calibration + evaluation segments (Obsidian's convention). The daily simulator remains only for the raw-sleeve diagnostic.
- Parity: on a mirror of the bucket built from Obsidian's cache, the reference illiquidity sleeve matches Obsidian's `_variant_driver` run bit-for-bit in calibration and to 0.05 pts over evaluation (OI cap-leg timing). Memory: panels built year by year; ~3.5 GB peak on 650 names.
